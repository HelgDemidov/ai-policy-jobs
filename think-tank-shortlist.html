Emerging Tech / AI Policy — institution shortlist

Scope: think tanks & international-org research units, AI/security/governance policy — not technical standardization Roles: analyst → director, PM optional Employment: full-time preferred, part-time OK Languages: EN + RU only
What changed since v3 — correction, not just an update
  • I had it wrong on NATO. v2/v3 said "Montenegro is a NATO member, you qualify" — that's true only for a Montenegro national. With RF citizenship, NATO membership eligibility doesn't transfer just from living there. NATO's founding purpose is collective defense against Russia specifically — this isn't a soft case, it's now moved to excluded, alongside RAND.
  • Same logic caught something I'd also silently dropped in the v1→v2 rewrite: Council of Europe (Strasbourg) — re-added below, but Russia was formally expelled as a CoE member state in March 2022, so this is a materially different, more fraught case than my original "ideal fit" framing suggested.
  • The emigrant/no-return framing does move some Tier C entries — in the other direction. A security-clearance requirement is a categorical US-citizenship bar; four years abroad doesn't change that (RAND, CNAS, SCSP, CSIS's Wadhwani Center, NDIA ETI stay excluded). But a research mission that frames Russia as the adversary (Hoover, the Alperovitch Institute, AEI's Foreign & Defense Policy studies) is a judgment call about the person, not a citizenship-gated rule — and a credible, openly Kremlin-critical Russian emigre analyst is an established, even valued, profile at exactly this kind of institution. Moved those three from "excluded" to "reconsider."
  • UN-system bodies (UNESCO, UNIDIR, OECD/GPAI, EU AI Office) are unaffected — Russia hasn't been expelled from the UN or the EU never included it, so nothing changes there beyond what was already flagged.

Tier C United States

Re-researched in full this round. Organized by exclusion mechanism, not just by name — that's the useful axis given the citizenship constraint. "Excluded" below means strong circumstantial signal, not a published statement — no US org publishes "we won't hire Russians," for obvious legal reasons, so this is inferred from mission framing and clearance/contract exposure, not confirmed verbatim.

Hard exclude — citizenship-gated security clearance

This is a categorical bar, not a judgment call: clearance eligibility requires US citizenship as step one, before any individual assessment even starts. Time abroad or a no-return intent don't touch this — only naturalization would, and that's a decade-plus path.

RAND CorporationSanta Monica / DCexcluded
Your own reference case — extensive classified DoD/IC contract portfolio, most research roles require US citizenship and clearance eligibility as a baseline.
CNAS — Center for a New American SecurityWashington, DClikely excluded
Core mission is US defense/national-security policy with deep DoD-alumni staffing and closed-door Pentagon engagement; the profile matches RAND closely enough to treat as clearance-culture-gated by default.
SCSP — Special Competitive Studies ProjectWashington, DClikely excluded
Direct descendant of the National Security Commission on AI, staffed heavily from DoD/IC alumni and working close to classified USG assessments — the barrier here is staff-clearance culture, not just editorial stance.
CSIS — Wadhwani Center for AI and Advanced TechnologiesWashington, DCprogram-specific
This specific program explicitly prefers candidates with DoD/IC/DARPA/DIU/CDAO/IARPA background — other CSIS programs may be less restrictive, but this one is not.
NDIA Emerging Technologies Institute (ETI)Arlington, VAexcluded
Carried over — this is a defense-industry trade association's own policy hub, working directly on Pentagon acquisition; same risk category as RAND, arguably more so.

Reconsider — mission framing isn't a hire-bar for a credible emigrant/critic profile

Moved out of "excluded" given the emigrant/no-return framing. A research mission that treats Russia as the adversary is a judgment call about you specifically, not a citizenship-gated rule the way a clearance is — and a Kremlin-critical Russian emigre analyst is a normal, sometimes valued, profile at exactly this kind of institution. Individual roles may still need a clearance for other reasons; lead with the emigrant/critic framing explicitly rather than leaving it for them to guess.

Hoover InstitutionStanford, CAreconsider
Runs an "International Working Group on Russian Sanctions" arguing for tighter tech-export denial to Russia — that's a policy stance about the Russian state, not a bar on Russian nationals as such. Verify per role whether it touches classified material.
Alperovitch Institute (Johns Hopkins SAIS)Washington, DCreconsider
Founder Dmitri Alperovitch is himself a Russian-born emigre who left and became a prominent Kremlin critic — the institute's whole existence is a data point that this profile is not just tolerated but can be a credential here, not a disqualifier.
AEI — Foreign and Defense Policy StudiesWashington, DCreconsider
Hawkish, ongoing Russia/Ukraine-war analysis — but that's an editorial stance, not a citizenship bar. AEI's separate Technology & Innovation (domestic-economic) program never carried this signal in the first place — see "open" list.

Tier C Japan

Researched from scratch. The same pattern as the US shows up even more starkly: government/ministry-linked institutes are hard excludes (Japan fully joined US/EU sanctions and export controls on Russia after 2022, on top of its own long-running Kuril Islands dispute with Russia), and the genuinely independent options are thin on AI-specific programming. Unlike the US "reconsider" bucket above, the emigrant/no-return framing probably doesn't move these much — Japanese ministry-linked institutes run on civil-service secondment norms and categorical nationality rules, not the individual-credibility judgment calls that a US think tank like Hoover or Alperovitch can make. Add a general caveat on top of everything below: most substantive roles at Japanese institutions expect working Japanese, even at internationally-facing ones — not confirmed per-org, but worth assuming.

National Institute for Defense Studies (NIDS)Tokyoexcluded
This isn't a think tank with government ties — it is the Ministry of Defense's own in-house research institute, staffed by MOD civilian officials and Self-Defense Force officers. The clearest Japan analogue to RAND.
Nakasone Peace Institute (NPI)Tokyoexcluded
MOFA-funded (Ministry of Foreign Affairs), research staff "mostly seconded from various Japanese government ministries," with a live research line on China/Russia information-warfare and generative-AI use in cognitive warfare.
Japan Institute of International Affairs (JIIA) — Center for Disarmament, Science and TechnologyTokyolikely excluded
MOFA-linked, founded at a former PM's initiative, explicit mandate to study "technological innovations changing the nature of warfare" for Japan's security — same quasi-governmental profile as Nakasone.
Sasakawa Peace Foundation — National Security and Japan-U.S. ProgramTokyo (+ SPF-USA, Washington DC)program-specific
This specific unit is built around the Japan-US security alliance — high sensitivity by the same logic as the US defense cluster. The Foundation is large and diverse; other programs (ocean policy, general international exchange) weren't researched and may carry less of this signal.
RIETI — Research Institute of Economy, Trade and IndustryTokyoambiguous
Technically a quasi-governmental "incorporated administrative agency," but explicitly describes itself as hosting "leading Japanese and prominent foreign researchers" and is genuinely open on paper. Its "economic security" research line could still carry sensitivity — best Japan candidate found, but an imperfect topical fit (trade/economic policy, not security/AI governance per se) and not confirmed for your specific case.
The Genron NPOTokyoweak topic fit
Genuinely independent (not government-seconded staff), lower citizenship-risk than the rest of this list on structural grounds alone — but no confirmed AI/tech-policy programme was found; its focus is general democracy/international-dialogue convening (it runs the Tokyo Conference).
Not carried forward: CAIDP (Center for AI and Digital Policy, Washington DC) — on closer look it's structured around academic-credit externships and volunteer fellows, not paid analyst/researcher/director roles, so it doesn't fit the position criteria this round. Kept out rather than force-fit.
